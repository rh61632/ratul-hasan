The Journey & On-Site Experience

The day began at 7:30 am on the JUST campus with an opening photoshoot under a beautifully cloudy sky that set an enthusiastic mood for the journey ahead. With 37 members—including 7 female engineers and 3 dedicated faculty members—the delegation boarded the tour bus, where breakfast was served on route. The scenic ride from Jashore to Khulna was accompanied by gentle monsoon drizzles, vibrant discussions, and light-hearted bonding.

Upon arrival at the Khulna 330 MW Power Plant in Khalishpur, the delegation was accorded exceptional hospitality by the plant authority, treated with the warmth and respect of honored academic guests rather than conventional visitors.

Reflections from the Delegation: "We came back with not only some photographs, but also enormous experience and learning. Apart from technical learning, the day was also filled with moments of bonding. From enjoying meals together to taking memorable group photos that captured our unity and enthusiasm—the tour was totally worth it. It was about experiencing, asking, networking and understanding the world where our engineering knowledge is applied."

A key highlight was meeting Md. Zahid Hossain, Chief Engineer of the plant, who spent valuable personal time with the delegation, discussing operational infrastructure, grid stabilization challenges, and maintenance protocols. As a gesture of institutional gratitude, the IEEE JUST SB delegation presented a commemorative crest to the Chief Engineer before concluding the site visit at 2:30 pm and stopping for lunch at Golpata Rosui Ghor at Khulna Zero Point.

Technical Architecture & Engineering Insights

During the walk-through led by BPDB engineers, the delegation inspected each critical phase of the combined-cycle power block:

Combined Cycle (Brayton-Rankine)

Examined how exhaust gases from the high-temperature gas turbine are channeled directly into a Heat Recovery Steam Generator (HRSG) to power a steam turbine block without consuming additional fuel, achieving elevated thermal efficiency exceeding 52%.

Dual-Fuel Agility (Gas & Diesel)

Inspected the automated fuel transfer manifold designed to run primarily on natural gas while retaining full, automated capability to switch to high-speed diesel (HSD/HFO) during gas transmission line pressure drops, ensuring regional grid security.

Turbine Governors & Synchronizers

Studied electro-hydraulic turbine governors, automated synchro-check relays, and automatic voltage regulators (AVR) matching frequency, voltage amplitude, and phase angle before tying into the 230 kV national grid.

Central SCADA & DCS Control Room

Observed real-time telemetry screens monitoring bearing vibrations, rotor eccentricity, boiler steam pressures, and dispatch setpoints sent from the National Load Despatch Centre (NLDC).

Substation Switchyard & Industrial Safety

The outdoor switchyard inspection highlighted high-voltage protection and grid delivery equipment:

  • Step-Up Transformers (GSU): Stepping up generated voltage from 15.75 kV to 230 kV for long-distance national transmission with minimal transmission loss.
  • SF6 Gas Circuit Breakers: Advanced arc-quenching circuit breakers capable of interrupting massive short-circuit fault currents within milliseconds.
  • Cooling Towers & Water Treatment: Circulating cooling water systems incorporating demineralization (DM) plants to prevent turbine blade scaling and corrosion.
  • Safety Standards & Emergency Measures: Plant fire-suppression systems, transformer deluge valves, and emergency auxiliary diesel generators.
